# Bridgit Brand Voice Guide

This document is the source of truth for how Bridgit communicates with customers, prospects, and partners. The marketing-repurpose skill reads from this file via the `lookup_style_guide` Model Context Protocol tool.

## tone

We sound like a knowledgeable colleague, not a vendor pitching a product. We are:

- **Direct.** Short sentences. Strong verbs. We don't bury the point.
- **Specific.** We use construction-industry vocabulary, never marketing jargon. We say "crew", "subs", "schedule", "headcount", "field" — not "ecosystem" or "value proposition".
- **Consultative.** We ask questions before we make recommendations. We tell stories about how other contractors solved similar problems.
- **Respectful of the reader's time.** Long emails feel like they're hiding something. Three to five sentences for a follow-up. One screen for a one-pager.

## banned-phrases

Do not use any of the following — they make us sound like a generic SaaS company instead of a partner who understands construction:

- leverage, leveraging, leveraged
- synergize, synergy, synergies
- circle back (the noun usage)
- touch base
- thought leadership
- best-in-class, industry-leading, world-class
- deep dive
- ecosystem
- value proposition

## preferred-phrases

Use the words our customers use. These are the markers of someone who knows the industry:

- crew, crews
- schedule, scheduling
- subcontractor, subs
- project (preferred over "engagement" or "initiative")
- field, field operations, field crew
- headcount, headcount planning
- workforce, workforce planning
- general contractor, GC
- punch list, RFI, change order (use only when contextually appropriate)

## structure

For any **LinkedIn post**:
- 3-5 short sentences max.
- Open with a specific observation, not a claim.
- One question or call-to-action at the end.
- No emojis. No hashtags. No "👇" or "↓" pointing tricks.

For any **sales-enablement one-pager**:
- Headline at the top (one sentence, what is this).
- Three short sections: *The problem*, *How we solve it*, *Proof from a customer*.
- Maximum one page when printed.
- One concrete customer outcome with a real number (not "x% improvement").

For any **customer email**:
- Subject line under 60 characters.
- Open with a specific reference to the recipient's situation.
- One observation, one suggestion, one clear next step.
- Sign-off: "Thanks, <name>".
- Length: 3-5 sentences. Anything longer should be a separate document.
